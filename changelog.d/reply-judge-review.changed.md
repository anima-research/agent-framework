- **The reply judge (`judge` wake rules), which 0.28.0 shipped, answers its first review:**
  - A judge call whose request the framework refuses to send (a hold on the agent's provider calls or on the judge's model, a surgery's hold on the store, a stopping framework) is now counted in the rule's `judgeState.verdicts.refused`, traced as `gate:judge-verdict` with verdict `refused`, the hold's words (`hold`, on one line, notice openings marked, cut to 300 characters) and its stated end (`until`), and named in gate_status while it lasts, with what judge rules do meanwhile. It stays out of the three failures in a row, and gate_status adds "refusals aside" to its word on them when one falls inside them. In 0.32.0 it was counted and traced nowhere.
  - Under `onError: "skip"`, a refused call's batch is now held in memory, with any batch that arrives while the hold's stated wait lasts (a rule keeping its newest 100 events, as it does while a turn holds them), and judged once the wait ends, or sooner when an operator releases a provider wait or a surgery ends. In 0.32.0 it was let go. A hold that stated no end, or no time left, keeps its held batches, and those held for a turn, through turns' ends until a batch that arrives with no turn running is asked, taking its rule's held batch in with it, and its call gets a verdict, or the framework lets the agent's calls go again, at an acceleration hold's end or an operator's or a surgery's release; one that lifts unannounced leaves them held until then. A call that fails lets its batch go, with the held events it took in, as a failed call's batch goes under "skip". A refusal reported after a later-asked call's verdict, or after a release, doesn't stand. `EventGate.upcomingWakes` lists a judge rule's batch due before a stated wait ends as held for the judge until that end, and past it while a turn or the host's quiesce holds it, which the entry says while one does.
  - Under `onError: "wake"`, while a refusal's stated wait lasts, each judge rule's batch is delivered without the judge being asked, since each ask compiles the agent's context only to be refused. A batch that a running turn or the host's quiesce holds past the wait's end goes to the judge then, and so does one that waits behind a call of its rule that the judge answers WAIT when its calls are no longer refused: once the wait has ended or a release has ended the refusal, or when that call, asked after the refused one, ends the refusal with that WAIT. A batch that takes in its rule's held batch keeps the newest 100 events of the two, as a held batch does. `EventGate.upcomingWakes` lists a judge rule's batch due before that wait ends as delivered without the judge, with `certain: true`, counting only its events whose channel isn't held when the list is made, unless the judge is deciding one of the rule's batches, or a turn that changes what the judge reads runs or the host is quiesced, when the list is made. Then it's listed with `certain: false`, as one that only may be, and its entry says what would send it to the judge. A batch held for a turn while that wait lasts is listed as held for the judge, or delivered without it if the judge's calls are still refused when the turn ends.
  - When the agent's last turn failed, with its retries spent or by a throw before its stream (at its checkpoint, locus resolution or typing indicator), and no turn has started since, the judge is told first that what woke that turn may be unanswered, and whether a wake for the agent is already queued, is held until a provider hold ends or is released, or nothing runs the turn again. In 0.32.0 it was told of the failed turn only by any `[inference-failed]` marker in the context and, while a re-arm was held, by that wake among the others: "a turn whose retries ran out, sent again (1 of 3)".
  - What participants wrote is quoted, so it can't make the judge document's structure: `&`, `<` and `>` are escaped as entities, each line of a message or an event after its first is indented, a `[` that opens a line of a message's text, of a tool's result or of an event, after any spaces or invisible or control characters, is escaped with a backslash, while the message's own block labels (`[calls …]`, `[… returned]`) aren't, and a name, a channel or an author id is kept to one line, with its square brackets escaped. An event's channel and its author are each quoted as a JSON string, so neither can read as the other. An event's "(addresses the agent)" marker comes right after its time, ahead of its channel and author.
  - An `UpcomingWake` without `at`, in a judge request or from `EventGate.upcomingWakes`, is no longer always due as soon as the agent is free. One with `when` is due when that says, such as events buffered until no turn runs and the host isn't quiesced, a batch held for the judge, or a batch the judge is deciding, and `EventGate.upcomingWakes` lists those after the wakes with a time. In 0.32.0 every wake without `at` was due as soon as the agent was free, so a judge function a host gives an `EventGate` it constructs (the constructor's `judge` option) that shows one that way shows these wrongly. The framework's own judge shows `when` in its place.
  - A request the provider refuses as too long is asked once more, from a fresh window, at one character a token, the lowest ratio the estimate takes, unless it was built at that already. Refused again, it fails as any failed call does. The refusal leaves the estimate as it was: it follows responses, the second request's among them. In 0.32.0 such a refusal failed the call and left the ratio as it was, so later requests were built at that ratio until a response corrected it.
  - For an agent with the judge wired, `wake_remove_rule`'s description now says in its first sentence that a judge rule's pending batches still go where the rule as it was sends them, where 0.32.0 says in its last that such a batch still goes to the judge, by the rule as it was. Under `onError: "wake"`, while the judge's calls are refused, this change delivers such a batch without the judge. A tool list that shows a tool by its first sentence alone, as the framework's `settings` toolset does, shows it. For such an agent, `wake_add_rule`'s `judge` parameter now says that when the judge says wait, nothing more is added to the agent's context, and what was stored as it arrived stays stored for when the agent is next woken, where 0.32.0 says the events stay in its context. The two changed descriptions change such an agent's tool definitions once, which is one prompt-cache miss. The judge's own system prompt changes the same way, so each agent's judge requests miss their prompt cache until one of them has written it.
