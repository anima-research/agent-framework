- **The reply judge (`judge` wake rules), as 0.28.0 shipped it, answers its first review:**
  - A judge call the framework refuses without making it (a hold on the agent's provider calls or on the judge's model, a surgery's hold on the store, a stopping framework) is now counted in the rule's `judgeState.verdicts.refused`, traced as `gate:judge-verdict` with verdict `refused`, the hold's words (`hold`) and its stated end (`until`), and named in gate_status while it lasts, with what judge rules do meanwhile. It stays out of the three failures in a row. In 0.28.0 it was counted and traced nowhere.
  - Under `onError: "skip"`, a refused call's batch is now held in memory, with any batch that arrives while the hold's stated wait lasts, and judged once the wait ends (at least a second on), or sooner when an operator releases a provider wait or a surgery ends. In 0.28.0 it was let go. A hold that stated no end keeps its held batches, and those held for a turn, through turns' ends until a batch that arrives with no turn running is asked, taking its rule's held batch in with it, and its call gets a verdict, or the hold is released; one that lifts unannounced leaves them held until then. A refusal reported after a later-asked call's verdict, or after a release, doesn't stand.
  - Under `onError: "wake"`, while a refusal's stated wait lasts, each judge rule's batch wakes the agent without the judge being asked, since each ask compiles the agent's context only to be refused.
  - When the agent's last turn failed with its retries spent and no turn has completed since, the judge is told first that what woke that turn may be unanswered, and whether a wake for the agent is already queued, is held until a provider hold ends or is released, or nothing runs the turn again. In 0.28.0 it was told nothing beyond any `[inference-failed]` marker in the context.
  - What participants wrote is quoted, so it can't make the judge document's structure: `&`, `<` and `>` are escaped as entities, each line of a message or an event after its first is indented, and a name, a channel or an author id is kept to one line, with its square brackets escaped. An event's "(addresses the agent)" marker comes right after its time, ahead of its channel and author.
  - A request the provider refuses as too long is asked once more, from a fresh window, with the ratio it was built at halved, to no less than one character a token, unless it was built at one already; refused again, it fails as any failed call does. The lower ratio stays for later requests until responses correct it. In 0.28.0 such a refusal failed the call, and every later request was built at the same ratio.
