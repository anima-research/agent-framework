- **The reply judge (`judge` wake rules), which 0.28.0 shipped, answers its first review:**
  - A judge call whose request the framework refuses to send (a hold on the agent's provider calls or on the judge's model, a surgery's hold on the store, a stopping framework) is now counted in the rule's `judgeState.verdicts.refused`, traced as `gate:judge-verdict` with verdict `refused`, the hold's words (`hold`, on one line, notice openings marked, cut to 300 characters) and its stated end (`until`), and named in gate_status on one line while it lasts, with its stated end and, under `"skip"`, how many events are held for the judge. It stays out of the three failures in a row, and doesn't end them. In 0.33.0 it was counted and traced nowhere.
  - Under `onError: "skip"`, a refused call's batch is now held in memory, with any batch that arrives while the hold's stated wait lasts (a rule keeping its newest 100 events, as it does while a turn holds them), and goes back to the judge once the wait ends, or sooner when an operator releases a provider wait or a surgery ends, once no turn that changes what the judge reads runs and the host isn't quiesced. In 0.33.0 it was let go. A hold that stated no end, or no time left, keeps its held batches, and those held for a turn, through turns' ends until a judge call gets a REPLY or WAIT, such as that of a batch that arrives while no turn that changes what the judge reads runs and the host isn't quiesced, which takes its rule's held batch in with it, or the framework lets the agent's calls go again, at an acceleration hold's end or an operator's or a surgery's release; one that lifts unannounced leaves them held until then. A call that fails lets its batch go, with the held events it took in, as a failed call's batch goes under "skip". Each refusal stands, whichever call it came from, until the next refusal takes its place, its stated end, the next REPLY or WAIT, or a release, so one reported late holds a batch as one reported on time would. `EventGate.upcomingWakes` lists a judge rule's batch due before a stated wait ends as held for the judge until that end, and past it while a turn or the host's quiesce holds it, which the entry says while one does.
  - Under `onError: "wake"`, while a refusal's stated wait lasts, each judge rule's batch is delivered without the judge being asked, since each ask compiles the agent's context only to be refused. A batch that a turn changing what the judge reads, or the host's quiesce, holds past the wait's end goes to the judge then, and so does one that waits behind a call of its rule that the judge answers WAIT, since a WAIT ends the refusal. A batch that takes in its rule's held batch keeps the newest 100 events of the two, as a held batch does. `EventGate.upcomingWakes` still lists a judge rule's batch due before that wait ends as going to the judge, with `certain: false`.
  - When the agent's last turn failed, with its retries spent or by a throw before its stream (at its checkpoint, locus resolution or typing indicator), and no turn has started since, the judge is told first that what woke that turn may be unanswered, and what runs it again, by the kinds of wake in its list: a wake already queued, a turn a provider hold keeps until its end, or nothing. In 0.33.0 it was told of the failed turn only by any `[inference-failed]` marker in the context and, while a re-arm was held, by that wake among the others: "a turn whose retries ran out, sent again (1 of 3)".
  - What participants wrote is quoted, so it can't make the judge document's structure: `&`, `<` and `>` are escaped as entities, each line of a message or an event after its first is indented, a `[` that opens a line of a message's text, of a tool's result or of an event, after any spaces or invisible or control characters, is escaped with a backslash, while the message's own block labels (`[calls …]`, `[… returned]`) aren't, and a name, a channel or an author id is kept to one line, with its square brackets escaped, and a tool's name its backslashes. An event's channel and its author are each quoted as a JSON string, so neither can read as the other. An event's "(addresses the agent)" marker comes right after its time, ahead of its channel and author.
  - An `UpcomingWake` without `at`, in a judge request or from `EventGate.upcomingWakes`, is no longer always due as soon as the agent is free. One with `when` is due when that says, such as events buffered until no turn runs and the host isn't quiesced, a batch held for the judge, or a batch the judge is deciding, and `EventGate.upcomingWakes` lists those after the wakes with a time. In 0.33.0 every wake without `at` was due as soon as the agent was free, so a judge function a host gives an `EventGate` it constructs (the constructor's `judge` option) that shows one that way shows these wrongly. The framework's own judge shows `when` in its place. `healthSnapshot()`'s `upcomingWakes` leaves out a wake whose time is given in words.
  - A request the provider refuses as too long is asked once more, from a fresh window, at one character a token, the lowest ratio the estimate takes, unless it was built at that already. Refused again, it fails as any failed call does. The refusal leaves the estimate as it was: it follows responses, the second request's among them. The retry's window is anchored where it starts, as any request's window is, so later requests show the conversation from there, which fit, until the window from there outgrows their budget or its first messages are no longer found, and a fresh one is built. In 0.33.0 such a refusal failed the call and left the ratio as it was, so later requests were built at that ratio until a response corrected it.
  - For an agent with the judge wired, `wake_remove_rule`'s description now says in its first sentence that a judge rule's pending batches still go where the rule as it was sends them, where 0.33.0 says in its last that such a batch still goes to the judge, by the rule as it was. Under `onError: "wake"`, while the judge's calls are refused, this change delivers such a batch without the judge. A tool list that shows a tool by its first sentence alone, as the framework's `settings` toolset does, shows it. For such an agent, `wake_add_rule`'s `judge` parameter now says that when the judge says wait, nothing more is added to the agent's context, and what was stored as it arrived stays stored for when the agent is next woken, where 0.33.0 says the events stay in its context. The two changed descriptions change such an agent's tool definitions once, which is one prompt-cache miss. The judge's own system prompt changes the same way, so each agent's judge requests miss their prompt cache until one of them has written it.
  - `ReplyJudge.buildRequest(request)` with no `now` tells the question's times against the clock read once the agent's preview and the wakes waiting for it have been read, where 0.33.0 read it when called, before the preview. A host that calls it without `now` gets times as of the request's build, not as of the call.
- **A pending batch the gate couldn't deliver to any of its agents** is still named in a `[gate]` log line and dropped, and the line now quotes the error's text as a JSON string, so a line break in it starts no line of its own: `(no agent to deliver to)` becomes `("no agent to deliver to")`.
- **Three `[gate]` log lines say what happened in new words.** A judge call the framework refused to send was logged as `the reply judge made no call (…), so its batch of N events wakes the agent` (or `doesn't wake the agent`), and is now logged as `the reply judge's call was refused (…), so its batch of N events` and where the batch goes: `is held while the judge's calls are refused` under `"skip"`, or `goes to delivery without the judge` under `"wake"`. A judge rule's batch whose delivery throws before it wakes any agent was logged as `a judged batch of N events couldn't be delivered (…), so the batch is dropped`, and is now logged as `Rule "NAME": its pending batch of N events couldn't be delivered ("…"), so the batch is dropped`. A judge call that fails under `onError: "skip"` was logged as `the reply judge failed (…), so its batch of N events doesn't wake the agent (judge onError "skip")`, and is now logged as `… so its batch of N events is dropped without waking the agent (judge onError "skip")`, since that batch may hold events that waited while the judge's calls were refused, and they're dropped with it.
