- **Workspace mounts:** a mount root found on another device with the same inode and birth time is now the directory disk last agreed with. The workspace records it on its new device and keeps what it recorded about its files, so a reboot that gives the volume another device number, as macOS can, no longer leaves the mount taking no writes until a `sync` with `acceptRoot`, after which every draft whose bytes differed from disk's came back as a conflict. A root recorded before this release gains its birth time at the next pass or push that finds it on its old device and inode. A root on another device with another inode or another birth time, or with no birth time to compare (where Node reports none, or a record that hasn't gained one yet), still needs `acceptRoot`, as before. A block-level copy of the volume (an APFS clone, a `dd` or `asr` image) mounted at the path is read as the original: its older files are taken as disk edits, and files it lacks as deleted. The `sync` tool's description and its `acceptRoot` argument's say so.
