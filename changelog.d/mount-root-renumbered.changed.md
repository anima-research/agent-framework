- **Workspace mounts:** a mount root found on another device with the same inode and the same birth time, given to a fraction of a second, is now the directory disk last agreed with. The workspace records it on its new device and keeps what it recorded about its files, so a reboot that gives the volume another device number, as macOS can, no longer leaves the mount taking no writes until a `sync` with `acceptRoot`, after which every draft whose bytes differed from disk's came back as a conflict. A root recorded before this release gains its birth time at the next pass, or the next push that writes, that finds it on its old device and inode. A root on another device with another inode or another birth time, or with no birth time to compare (where the runtime reports none, or a record that hasn't gained one yet), or one in whole seconds, still needs `acceptRoot`, as before: volume roots made in the same second can share their inode and a whole-second birth time. A mounted APFS snapshot or a block-level replica of the volume (a `dd` or `asr` image) at the path keeps the original's inode and birth time, so it is read as the original: its older files are taken as disk edits and files it lacks as deleted, and a draft over one of them is a conflict.
