- A new wake-rule behavior, `judge`, that asks a small model before waking the agent. A judge rule batches matching events as a debounce does (`debounceMs`, with an optional `maxWaitMs` that judges a batch by then even while messages keep coming). When the batch fires, the reply judge reads three things: the agent's context as one document (`previewActivation`'s dry run of the request the agent would send if woken now, without what modules' `gatherContext` and MCPL servers' `beforeInference` would inject: its newest part, oldest first), the agent's own `instructions` to it, and the batch. It answers `REPLY` or `WAIT`. On reply the batch wakes the agent as a debounce's would. On wait it doesn't, and the events still reach its context: MCPL's channel and push events are in it from their arrival, and the content of any other event the rule matched is stored without a wake. (A `defer` rule stores nothing for such an event.) Rules are read in order, as ever: a mention rule placed ahead of a judge rule wakes the agent without the judge, and the turn it starts stops and holds the judge as any resident's turn does. A mention the judge rule itself matches goes to the judge with its batch, marked as addressing the agent.
- Batches are judged against the context as it stands after the residents' turns, since the residents share the message slot the judge reads. A batch that fires while a resident's turn runs or the host is quiesced is held, then judged once both are clear. A resident's turn that starts while the judge decides, or a quiesce, stops the judge, and its batch is judged once the turn ends or at resume, against what the turn left, and the turn never waits for the judge. A stopped call is no verdict: it wakes nothing and isn't counted as a failure. A fork's or the subconscious's turn, in a store of its own, neither holds a judge rule's batch nor stops the judge; the wake a batch gets still waits while any agent's turn runs, as every batched wake does. A judge rule's batch that is waiting, held or being judged when the rule is changed or removed is still judged: by the rule as it is when the judge is asked, while it's still a judge rule, and by the rule as it was once it isn't. `wake_remove_rule` says so where a judge is wired. What a rule holds for the judge while a turn runs, and the events waiting behind a judge call, are each capped at the inference buffer's 100 events, dropping the oldest, which `inferenceDiagnostics()` counts.
- The judge is told what waiting would leave the agent with: what will wake it anyway with no new event, soonest first. That covers its own `skip_reply` timer, the end of a sleep, every pending batch but the one it's asked about (a debounce's; only "maybe" a judge rule's, whether collecting, being decided or held, and what a held one waits for; and events buffered behind a running turn), a wake for the agent already queued, and one a provider hold keeps until the hold ends or is released. It lists eight, soonest first, and says how many more there are and how many of those are only "maybe"; each one's words are quoted as participant text is. When the agent's last turn failed with its retries spent and no turn has completed since, it's told first that what woke that turn may be unanswered, and whether a wake for the agent is already queued, is held until a provider hold ends or is released, or nothing runs the turn again. Neither of the first two says a turn will start: the host's inference policy can still decline the wake. When none is pending, it's told nothing the host knows of is scheduled; a server's own timers, such as a heartbeat, aren't known to the host. The gate's part is `EventGate.upcomingWakes()` and `JudgeRequest.upcomingWakes`; the framework adds its own through `ReplyJudgeDeps.upcomingWakes` and `ReplyJudgeDeps.failedLastTurn`.
- The judge is wired unless the host turns it off (`GateOptions.judge.enabled: false`), and it runs only for judge rules, so a gate without them makes no judge calls. The agent writes judge rules with `wake_add_rule`'s new `judge` parameter. With the judge off, the tool is as it was. gate_status shows each judge rule's verdict counts and its newest verdict, with the judge's reason. It also says when a judge rule is in force with no judge wired, when the judge has failed three times in a row, and when the judge's calls are refused under a hold: until when, and what judge rules do meanwhile. Each verdict is traced as `gate:judge-verdict`, with its counts and tokens but not the judge's reason, which is written from the agent's context; a refused call is traced with verdict `refused` and its hold.
- The judge is `ReplyJudge` (`src/gate/reply-judge.ts`), configured by `GateOptions.judge`. The package root exports it with the judge's types, `JudgeEventSummary` among them:
  - `model`: default `claude-haiku-5-5`.
  - `maxInputTokens`: default 90,000, under the 100K line past which Haiku 5.5 costs five times as much. It's met by an estimate: until a response has corrected the estimate's ratio, a request on text denser than three characters a token (code, or Chinese, Japanese or Korean text) can carry more, and so can one whose newest message alone is over the budget, since that is always shown. A request the provider refuses as too long is asked once more, from a fresh window, with the ratio it was built at halved, to no less than one character a token, unless it was built at one already; refused again, it fails as any failed call does. The lower ratio stays for later requests until responses correct it. At least 4,000, which makes it read a short tail; a smaller value is read as 4,000, with a log line.
  - `timeoutMs`: default 20,000.
  - `onError`: `"wake"` (the default) or `"skip"`.
  - `effort`: default `"low"`; `"none"` sends none, for a provider without Anthropic's `output_config`.
  - `cacheTtl`: `"5m"` (the default) or `"1h"`. On Bedrock, membrane drops it and Bedrock's default applies.
  - `enabled: false` leaves it unwired.
- How the judge calls its model:
  - The agent's provider holds bind its calls, but where compression waits out a hold, a judge call is refused without being made: under a hold on the agent or on the call's model, and, as compression's are, under a surgery or at stop. A refused call is counted and traced as a refusal, apart from failures, and doesn't count toward the three failures in a row. Under `onError`'s `"wake"`, its batch wakes the agent, and while the hold's stated wait lasts, each judge rule's batch wakes it without the judge being asked. Under `"skip"`, its batch is held in memory, with any batch that arrives while that wait lasts, and judged once the wait ends (at least a second on), or sooner when an operator releases a provider wait or a surgery ends. A hold that stated no end keeps its held batches, and those held for a turn, through turns' ends until a batch that arrives with no turn running is asked, taking its rule's held batch in with it, and its call gets a verdict, or the hold is released; one that lifts unannounced, as recorded provider waits read again after they couldn't be, leaves them held until then. A refusal reported after a later-asked call's verdict, or after a release, doesn't stand: under `"skip"` its batch is held for a refusal that does, or asked again at once when none does. A call that fails records the wait its provider states, if any, so the judge's next call on that model is refused until it ends, and holds nothing else: the judge usually runs on another model than the agent's. None of them waits on the agent's turn, which stops the judge instead.
  - Each request's usage counts as the judged agent's, in its usage tally and the session's totals, as a turn's does.
  - It asks for membrane's native formatter: an XML-formatted request would end in a prefill, which current Claude models refuse. A membrane whose own formatter is OpenAI Responses', on that adapter outside its subscription mode, keeps its formatter whatever a request asks.
  - It reads the newest part of the agent's context that fits `maxInputTokens`, so the judge weighs the room's history and not only its last few lines. Input tokens are estimated at a ratio corrected from every response's reported usage.
  - The document goes in chunks from a fixed first message, with a cache breakpoint after the last full chunk, so successive requests read most of it from the prompt cache. The agent's system prompt (abridged) follows the conversation, and the batch's listing is capped at a tenth of the budget: it keeps the newest events, shown oldest first, and always the newest one.
  - What participants wrote is quoted, so it can't make the document's structure: `&`, `<` and `>` are escaped as entities in the document and the batch, each line of a message or an event after its first is indented, and a name, a channel or an author id is kept to one line, with its square brackets escaped.
  - The verdict is the last line in the asked-for form (`REPLY:` / `WAIT:` in capitals).
- Failures and fallbacks:
  - A judge that fails, times out or answers without a verdict wakes the agent (fail open), unless `onError` is `"skip"`.
  - Without a judge wired, a judge rule wakes the agent as a debounce of the same quiet period would.
