- The eager reference fetch, which fetches an MCPL tool result's small references before the agent sees the result, now keeps to its own limit (#747). A fetch it starts reads no further than the first chunk past the server's eager limit (`autofetch.maxBytes`, 256 KiB by default). Before, only the size a reference claimed was held to that limit, so a body larger than its claim was read and saved whole, up to the host's 256 MiB ceiling. Once its fetch of a reference fails, for any reason, the eager fetch doesn't choose that reference again at later results, where it used to try at each: its stub offers `fetch_reference`, which still works. For a script's result, a reference that claims no more than the eager limit but whose body runs past it is now read twice: up to the limit by the eager fetch, then whole by the script's own fetch of its references, where it was read whole once.
- Each caller of the reference fetcher now waits no longer than its own timeout, counted from its own call and covering the workspace save: 10 s for the eager fetch, and 60 s for `fetch_reference` and a script's references. A caller that joined a fetch already in flight used to keep that fetch's timer and byte ceiling. So an eager fetch that joined a `fetch_reference` could hold the tool result for 60 s, and a `fetch_reference` that joined an eager fetch failed at its 10 s. Now, when the fetch it joined stops on a narrower byte limit than the caller's own, the caller fetches again under its own, and when it stops at its time limit, the caller fetches again if it has more time left than that fetch's starter's whole timeout. A fetch that succeeds still answers every caller waiting on it, an eager fetch that joined a wider one included.
