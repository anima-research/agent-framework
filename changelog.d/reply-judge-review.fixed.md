- **The reply judge (`judge` wake rules), where it didn't do what 0.28.0's notes on it say:**
  - 0.28.0's notes say a pending batch is judged "by the rule as it is when the judge is asked", but a batch pending when gate.json was reloaded was judged by its rule's instructions from before the edit. A batch is now judged by its rule as the rule is when the judge is asked, and a reload clears the changed rules' state and puts the new rules in force before it sends their pending batches.
  - 0.28.0's notes say the judge is told about "other rules' pending batches", but it wasn't told about other judge rules' batches being decided or held, or about events buffered behind a running turn. It's now told about every pending batch but the one it's asked about: besides what 0.28.0 listed, other judge rules' batches being decided or held (with what one held for a refusal waits for), as "maybe", and events buffered while a turn runs or the host is quiesced, which wake the agent once neither holds them. It lists eight, soonest first, and says how many more there are and how many of those are only "maybe". `EventGate.upcomingWakes` lists them too, leaving out a given rule's batches and buffered events.
- **Corrections to 0.28.0's notes on the judge:**
  - "the request the agent would send if woken now, `previewActivation`'s dry run": the dry run leaves out what modules' `gatherContext` and MCPL servers' `beforeInference` would inject.
  - "It always uses membrane's native formatter": it asks for it, and a membrane whose own formatter is OpenAI Responses', on that adapter outside its subscription mode, keeps its formatter whatever a request asks.
  - "`maxInputTokens`: default 90,000, under the 100K line": the budget is met by an estimate. Until a response has corrected the estimate's ratio, a request on text denser than three characters a token (code, or Chinese, Japanese or Korean text) can carry more, and so can one whose newest message alone is over the budget, since that is always shown.
  - "`cacheTtl`: `"5m"` (the default) or `"1h"`": on Bedrock, membrane drops it and Bedrock's default applies.
  - "On wait it doesn't, and the events stay in context, as for `defer`": the events are in the agent's context when they're stored as they arrive, as MCPL's channel and push events are, and as the framework's own modules store the message an event of theirs carries; a wait, like `defer`, adds nothing more.
